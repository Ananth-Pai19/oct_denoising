# TGV² decomposition denoising — reproduction of Duan et al. (2016)

Reproduction of

> J. Duan, W. Lu, C. Tench, I. Gottlob, F. Proudlock, N. N. Samani, L. Bai,
> **"Denoising optical coherence tomography using second order total generalized
> variation decomposition"**, *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control* 24 (2016) 120–127.
> doi:10.1016/j.bspc.2015.09.012

Everything here is FFT-based and CPU-only (single-threaded NumPy/SciPy). No training, no
deep learning, no GPU.

`scripts/02_tune_parameters.py` and `02b_baseline_tuning.py` must be run before
`03_*`/`04_*`, which read `results/tuning/chosen_parameters.json` and

sensitivity ≈ 12 min, tuning ≈ 6 min. A single 256×256 phantom denoise takes ≈ 0.7 s
(20 iterations) and a 540×960 B-scan ≈ 25 s.

## 3. What the method is (one paragraph)

The log-compressed OCT frame `f` is split into a piecewise-smooth structure `u` and an
oscillatory texture `div(g)`:

    min_{u,g,p}  ½‖f − u − div g‖² + β‖g‖₁ + α₁‖∇u − p‖₁ + α₀‖ε(p)‖₁ ,   ε(p)=½(∇p+∇pᵀ)

solved by a split-Bregman iteration with three split variables (`m≈g`, `w≈∇u−p`, `v≈ε(p)`),
three Bregman variables and three penalties (`λ₁,λ₂,λ₃`). Each of the `u`, `g`, `p`
sub-problems is a constant-coefficient linear system, diagonalised by the 2-D DFT, so one
iteration costs a handful of FFTs; `v, m, w` are pointwise isotropic soft-thresholds.

Interface:

```python
from src.tgv import tgv_decompose, vo_decompose
u, texture = tgv_decompose(f, alpha0=0.1, alpha1=0.1, beta=0.1,
                           lam1=2.0, lam2=2.0, lam3=2.0, max_iter=300)   # tol=1e-3
u_tv, tex_tv = vo_decompose(f, alpha1=0.1, beta=0.1, lam1=2.0, lam2=2.0)
u, tex, info = tgv_decompose(f, ..., return_info=True)   # + energies, per-iteration history
```

## 4. Headline results

See `report/report.md` for the full discussion and `results/` for every number.

* **Correctness.** `div = −gradᵀ` and `εᵀ = −div_sym` hold to 1e-15; the FFT closed forms
  for the `u`, `g` and `p` sub-problems reproduce an explicit sparse solve to ≤6e-16
  relative error; `TGV² → TV` as `α₀ → ∞` (max |u_TGV − u_VO| = 0.027 on [0,1]).
* **Synthetic (Table 2 analogue).** TGV² has the highest PSNR of all eight methods at every
  noise level (30.70/28.69/27.29/26.20/25.32 dB for σ² = 0.02…0.10) — the same ordering as
  the paper, about 1.3 dB below its absolute values at low noise and slightly above at
  σ² = 0.08.
* **Staircase.** On a piecewise-affine image, TV leaves 67% of in-ramp pixels locally flat
  (a staircase) and TGV² 2.3%, at *higher* PSNR (33.4 vs 32.6 dB) and SSIM (0.948 vs 0.922).
* **Real data.** Visual comparison in `results/figures/real/`; residual noise in flat blocks
  drops from 0.17 to 0.02–0.03 for both VO and TGV², and TGV² keeps the layer boundaries
  sharper without the plateaus VO produces.

## 5. Honest limitations (details in the report)

* The paper reports **no** parameter values (`α₀, α₁, β, λ₁–λ₃`) and **no** phantom
  parameters (`aᵢ, bᵢ, σᵢ`), so exact reproduction of Table 2 is not well posed. Parameters
  here are tuned once (σ²=0.04) and frozen; the phantom is a documented reconstruction.
* The paper's own RMSE column is inconsistent with its PSNR/SNR columns (see
  `report/paper_summary.md` §4).
* The baselines' free parameters are also unreported; they are tuned once, on the same
  image and noise level, so the comparison is as fair as we could make it — but the
  NCDF/ACED/HWT numbers should be read as "a reasonable implementation", not "theirs".
* No GPU: the paper mentions GPU-parallelisability but reports no runtime.
